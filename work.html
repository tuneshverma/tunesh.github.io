01 / Vision Group · Jul 2020 — Sep 2023

An autonomous cooler that watches what you take.

A vending cooler with no checkout. Scan to unlock, take what you want, close the door — the system works out which products actually left and charges for those.

Autonomous Retail · Smart Vending Cooler

From detection to a transaction you can trust

YOLO · Classification · Centroid tracking · DeepStream · Jetson

A customer scanned a barcode to unlock the cooler, opened the door, took one or more beverages, and closed it. The amount was deducted from their wallet automatically. The hard part was never spotting a bottle. It was working out what the customer picked up, whether it actually left the cooler, whether it came back, and which SKU was ultimately taken — because getting that wrong means charging someone for a drink they put back.

Training the detector

  • Captured a substantial dataset across camera viewpoints and operating conditions.
  • Covered angles from the top to the bottom of the cooler, varied lighting, and camera fogging.
  • Included partial obstruction, different ways customers hold bottles, and different product positions.
  • Videos were annotated by hand, then heavily augmented for robustness.
  • Reached 98%+ detection accuracy during internal testing.

Why two models, not one

A single detector trained on every client's SKUs would need retraining each time a catalogue changed, and would grow harder to train as products multiplied. Splitting the problem meant a new client only required a new classifier over the same detector.

Picked, returned, picked again

Detection and classification still aren't enough. A customer can lift a bottle, change their mind, put it back, and take a different one. A system that charges for whatever leaves the frame would bill them for both.

The hard part: one symptom, four suspects

The real engineering difficulty was raising end-to-end reliability, because every failure looks identical from the outside. A customer is charged the wrong amount — and nothing in that outcome tells you which stage broke.

Two deployments, one pipeline

The same DeepStream pipeline shipped in two configurations, and what differs between them is simply where the video is processed — and therefore what has to cross the network.

What this project actually was

Not "I trained a YOLO model". Detection, classification, tracking, temporal reasoning, edge deployment and remote model management, composed into a system that takes money from someone's wallet without a human in the loop. The interesting engineering lives in the seams between those parts, not inside any one of them.

What I took from it

That a pipeline's accuracy is not the product of its stages' accuracies — it is limited by whichever stage you cannot observe. Making failure attributable per component did more for reliability than any single model improvement.

The 98%+ figure is detection accuracy measured during internal testing, not end-to-end transaction accuracy. I don't have a separate measured number for the complete system, so I'm not claiming one — and latency, throughput and deployment counts are omitted for the same reason.
02 / MintM · Mar 2019 — Jan 2020

Retail intelligence from CCTV.

Two different systems: one converted existing CCTV streams into customer-behavior analytics; the other connected computer vision to a physical conveyor system for automated return processing.

Retail Footfall & Customer Behavior Analytics

Turning CCTV into structured retail data

YOLO · Tracking · ResNet · Heatmaps

The business objective was to transform existing CCTV infrastructure in retail stores into useful data for stakeholders: how many people enter and leave, which doors they use, visitor gender distribution, and where customers spend time inside the store.

Footfall & tracking

  • Used existing CCTV cameras and processed multiple camera streams.
  • YOLO detected people and centroid tracking maintained movement over time.
  • Virtual lines were placed around entry/exit regions to calculate IN/OUT counts.
  • A single camera could support multiple doors or entry/exit regions.
  • Tracking logic was strengthened to make counting more reliable.

Gender classification

  • Person bounding-box crops from the detector were collected and manually labeled.
  • Created roughly 5,000 male + 5,000 female samples.
  • Trained a ResNet-based classifier and integrated it with the detection pipeline.
  • Every detected person could be classified in parallel as male/female.
  • About 91% accuracy was observed on the deployed system during team testing.

Heatmaps & dwell behavior

Because each detected person had a tracked centroid, the same data could be used to measure spatial concentration and how long tracked people remained in particular areas. This enabled store heatmaps and customer-density analysis.

Why it mattered

The system moved beyond simple people counting. It created a data layer over existing video infrastructure so retail stakeholders could study traffic, demographics and customer movement patterns.

BigBasket · Return-to-Vendor

Automated barcode verification on a conveyor

Computer Vision · Image Processing · State Machine · Tracking

A real-time warehouse system for identifying returned products. Returned items were placed onto a conveyor, brought in front of a camera, scanned, and verified against the barcode/product dataset supplied by BigBasket.

Reliable barcode reading

  • Used barcode-reading libraries together with image-processing techniques.
  • Tracked the product through a defined virtual rectangular region.
  • The system continuously evaluated whether the barcode had been read successfully.

Closed-loop state machine

  • If the barcode was read, the item could continue forward after verification.
  • If reading failed, the conveyor could reverse and present the item to the camera again.
  • If it still failed, the conveyor could stop for a defined period to allow another read.
  • The computer-vision result directly controlled physical conveyor behavior.

Deployment

The system was deployed in a BigBasket warehouse. You visited the deployment environment to monitor accuracy and speed and validate behavior on real returned products.

Testing

Testing covered roughly 500 items. Barcode reading was reported at above 90% accuracy, with successful reads then verified against the client-provided dataset.

This project is an important early example of building a complete AI-enabled physical system—not just a model: perception → decision → state machine → machine control.
03 / Synergy Labs · Jun 2018 — Dec 2018

Two separate computer-vision POCs.

These projects were distinct and should remain separate in the portfolio: one focused on facial recognition and the other on document table understanding.

Facial Recognition

One-shot learning for office attendance

Triplet Loss · Face Recognition · Realtime Camera

The POC explored state-of-the-art facial-recognition approaches available at the time, including a triplet-loss approach associated with one-shot / single-shot learning.

Learning setup

  • Training used an anchor image, a positive image of the same person, and a negative image from a different person.
  • The objective was to learn a representation that separated same-person and different-person faces.
  • Approximately five images per employee were collected for enrollment.

Attendance workflow

A camera was placed in the office. Faces were recognized from the live stream and the recognition result was used to record attendance. This was a POC rather than a production-scale deployment.

Document Intelligence

Table detection & structured extraction

YOLO · Image Processing · Line Detection · Excel

Normal document extraction can work well for plain text, but complex tables require understanding the visual structure of rows, columns and cells. This POC focused on detecting tables and converting their contents into structured spreadsheet data.

Dataset & detection

  • Created and annotated roughly 3,000 table data points.
  • Used object detection techniques, including YOLO, to locate tables and relevant regions.
  • Built processing paths for tables with visible lines and tables without clear lines.

Extraction pipeline

  • Used image processing and line detection to reason about rows and columns.
  • Determined coordinates for relevant table regions/cells.
  • Extracted values from those coordinates and converted them into structured data.
  • Exported the resulting information into Excel sheets.
Built in 2018, this was an early document-AI problem tackled with computer vision, annotation, object detection and custom image-processing logic rather than today's much more readily available document/LLM tooling.
04 / Early AI experiments

Learning by building.

Before professional AI work, experimentation with image classification helped establish the foundations for model architecture, evaluation and generalization.

Blood Cell Classifier

Learning CNNs through image classification

Keras · CNN · InceptionV3 · Xception

Built an image-classification pipeline using the Kaggle Blood Cell Images dataset, with four classes: Eosinophil, Lymphocyte, Monocyte and Neutrophil.

What I explored

  • Built a CNN architecture from scratch.
  • Experimented with established architectures including InceptionV3 and Xception.
  • Used image augmentation and evaluated accuracy, loss and F1-style performance measures.

Learning objective

The goal was not only to maximize accuracy, but to understand how architecture choices, training behavior and generalization affect real model performance.
